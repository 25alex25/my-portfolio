Software Developer Portfolio

Project Proposal and Research

1. Website Purpose

The purpose of this website is to create a professional online portfolio that introduces me as an aspiring software developer and presents my education, developing technical skills, and programming projects in one organized place. The website will give potential employers a convenient way to learn about my background and review examples of what I can build.

2. Target Audience

The primary audience is recruiters, hiring managers, and software development employers who are considering candidates for internships or entry-level opportunities. A secondary audience includes instructors, classmates, and other technology professionals who may review my work or provide feedback.

3. Career Objective

My goal is to prepare for an entry-level software developer position. I want to continue strengthening my programming, problem-solving, and software development skills through coursework and practical projects. The portfolio will grow as I complete additional projects and gain experience.

4. Planned Portfolio Components

Research on software engineering portfolios emphasizes presenting a clear introduction, selected projects, organized technical skills, and easy-to-find contact information. Project descriptions should explain what was built, which technologies were used, and provide code or demonstration links when available (Intuit, 2026). Based on this research, the website will include:

  • Home: A brief introduction, career objective, and overview of the portfolio.
  • Projects: Selected coursework and personal programming projects, with descriptions, technologies used, and links to source code or demonstrations when available.
  • Skills: Technical skills and tools, including C++, HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, clearly identifying technologies that are still being learned.
  • Resume: Education, relevant coursework, technical qualifications, and experience as these are developed.
  • Contact: A professional email contact method and relevant professional profile links when available.

The Texas Tech University Department of Computer Science career guide recommends developing projects to improve technical ability and help demonstrate relevant interests and skills to employers. It also advises students to be prepared to explain their projects and how they relate to a position (Texas Tech University, 2025). For that reason, the Projects page will focus on work I can explain and describe accurately rather than listing projects without context.

5. Research Findings

The research supports building a portfolio that is easy to navigate and provides evidence of practical skills. Intuit recommends a concise professional introduction, a small selection of strong projects, a clearly organized skills section, and contact links. The Texas Tech University guide highlights the value of projects for developing skills and showing employers how a student's interests and experience connect to a role. These recommendations inform the planned pages and content of this website.

  1. Intuit. (2026, August 26). How to Build a Software Engineering Portfolio. https://www.intuit.com/blog/global-stories/software-engineer-portfolio/
  2. Texas Tech University, Department of Computer Science. (2025). Job & Internship Guide. https://www.depts.ttu.edu/cs/undergrad/documents/Career_Guide_PDFs/CS_Career_Guide_Updated_4.11.25.pdf

6. Planned Website Structure

The public portfolio will contain five main pages: Home, Projects, Skills, Resume, and Contact. A separate proposal.html page will document the project proposal and research. As required by the assignment, the proposal page will be published but will not appear in the website's main navigation.

Project direction: This portfolio is intended to represent a developing software developer's current abilities honestly and will be updated as new skills, coursework, and projects are completed.
